# Professional Reporting Skill

This skill transforms raw financial analysis into research-ready materials suitable for professional review and editing.

## Audit Compliance Language (Anti-Defamation Redlines)
To avoid legal risks and commercial defamation, all reports must use cautious, evidence-based language.
- **PROHIBITED TERMS**: "Fraud", "Fake", "Scam", "Cooking the books", "Embezzlement", "Lying", "Deception".
- **APPROVED ALTERNATIVES**:
  - Instead of "Fraud" $\rightarrow$ "Material inconsistency in financial reporting", "Significant accounting anomaly", "Aggressive revenue recognition".
  - Instead of "Lying" $\rightarrow$ "Discrepancy between management narrative and audited data".
  - Instead of "Fake" $\rightarrow$ "Unsubstantiated claim", "Lack of supporting evidence".
  - Instead of "Embezzlement" $\rightarrow$ "Unauthorized fund transfer", "Unexplained related-party outflow".
- **Rule**: Any definitive accusation of illegality must be accompanied by a direct quote from a regulatory body (e.g., SEC, CSRC) or a court judgment. Otherwise, use "suggests", "appears to be", or "indicates a need for further investigation".

## Report Structure
1. **Executive Summary**: Bottom-line conclusion first. Key findings, risk rating, and primary recommendation.
2. **Detailed Analysis**:
   - **Fact Section**: Verifiable data points with citations.
   - **Analysis Section**: Interpretation of the data, connecting the dots.
   - **Judgment Section**: Explicit "Bull/Bear" arguments and the agent's final stance.
3. **Evidence Appendix**: Full list of sources, page references, and calculation steps.
4. **Visualizations**: Descriptions of required charts (e.g., "Waterfall chart showing EBITDA bridge from 2022 to 2023").

## Writing Style
- **Objective & Neutral**: Avoid adjectives like "amazing" or "terrible". Use "significant increase" or "material decline".
- **Precise**: Use exact numbers. Instead of "about 10%", use "10.2%".
- **Structured**: Use bullet points, tables, and bold headers for scannability.

## Review Readiness
- Every judgment must be backed by at least one piece of evidence.
- All calculations must be reproducible from the provided steps.
